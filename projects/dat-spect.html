Abstract

Dopamine-transporter (DaT) SPECT imaging supports the diagnosis of parkinsonian syndromes, and the task is to classify each scan as normal or abnormal from the image alone. We built an end-to-end pipeline for the DrivenData DaT Parkinson's Challenge, which scores predictions by log loss on scans from several hospitals and scanner types.

Each scan is preprocessed on its own: masked, centred on the striatum and normalized by its own non-specific reference level, since intensities are not comparable across sites. Four CNN families (DatNet, a Siamese hemisphere model, SliceNet and a MedicalNet ResNet-10) are averaged and fused in logit space with a LightGBM model on 20 striatal binding-ratio features. All model selection uses site-grouped cross-validation.

The final submission reached an AUROC of 0.9379 and a private log loss of 0.3054, ranking 59th of 1,009 participants.

Keywords: DaT SPECT · Parkinson's disease · 3D CNN ensemble · striatal binding ratio · domain shift

Method

  1. Preprocess each scan on its own. Decode, mask and centre on the striatum, then divide by the scan’s own non-specific reference level, since intensity is not comparable across sites.
  2. Two grids. Resample to 96³ for the CNNs and 64³ for the binding-ratio head.
  3. Four CNN families. DatNet (3D CNN), a Siamese hemisphere comparison, SliceNet (2.5D) and a MedicalNet-initialised ResNet-10.
  4. Fuse. Average the CNNs and combine them with a LightGBM model on 20 striatal-binding-ratio features, weighted 0.8 to 0.2 in logit space.

Key findings

  • Final standing: the 008a submission ranked 59th on the private leaderboard with AUROC 0.9379.
  • CV vs leaderboard: each later model improved cross-validation but scored worse on the public leaderboard.
  • Every member counts: removing the weakest member, the binding-ratio head, costs 0.0055 log loss.
  • No calibration: cross-fitted temperature scaling measured worse, so none is applied.
