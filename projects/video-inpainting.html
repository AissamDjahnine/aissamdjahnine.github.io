Abstract

Inpainting spatio-temporal sequences is an active research topic that usually relies on supervision with large datasets. We reimplement an unsupervised approach that reconstructs missing regions in video sequences by training a generative model directly on occluded sequences.

A ResNet-type generator of 3D residual blocks with spatial self-attention completes each sequence, and only fills missing values while keeping observed pixels intact. Adversarial training with PatchGAN discriminators on individual frames and on whole sequences captures both per-frame detail and temporal coherence.

We train on geophysical data (sea surface temperature with cloud occlusions) and natural videos (FaceForensics++, KTH, BAIR) with raindrop, moving-bar and removed-pixel occlusions, evaluating with FID, FVD and mean absolute error.

Keywords: video inpainting · unsupervised learning · GAN · self-attention · PatchGAN · spatio-temporal data

Method

Let x and y be the true unknown and the occluded sequences. The goal is the most likely sequence given the observation, which splits into a prior on x and the likelihood of y given x.

  1. Prior. A generator G maps occluded sequences to completed ones; a random occlusion F with mask m̂ ~ pM re-masks the output, and adversarial training makes it indistinguishable from real occluded data.
  2. Likelihood. The generator only fills missing values and leaves observed pixels intact: G(y) = Gold(y) ⊙ m̄ + y ⊙ m.
  3. Generator. A ResNet-type network of 3D ResNet blocks followed by a spatial self-attention layer, encoding the masked sequence and decoding the reconstruction.
  4. Discriminators. PatchGAN discriminators run on individual frames (Df) and on whole sequences (Ds), classifying each N × N patch as real or fake.

Two supervised variants serve as references: a paired variant with corrupted–uncorrupted pairs (y, x), and an unpaired variant built from separate samples of each.

Results

Frames are evaluated with the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID, Inception features trained on ImageNet), sequences with the Fréchet Video Distance (FVD, I3D features trained on Kinetics-400), and reconstruction error with the mean absolute error.

Key findings

  • Hard problem: inpainting videos in a fully unsupervised context is a hard task and an active research area.
  • Training cost: training is very sensitive, not stable, and GPU- and time-consuming.
  • Frames and sequences: separate frame and sequence discriminators let the model capture both per-frame detail and temporal coherence.
