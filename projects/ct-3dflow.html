Unsupervised Pulmonary CT Analysis

3D Normalizing Flows

Aissam Djahnine, Alexandre Popoff, Emilien Jupin-Delevaux, Vincent Cottin, Olivier Nempont, Loic Boussel

CT-3DFlow · arXiv preprint · 2024

Paper arXiv All research

Overview

Can a model identify pulmonary abnormalities by learning what healthy CT scans look like? CT-3DFlow learns normal appearance from healthy 3D lung patches, then uses deviations from that distribution to classify a scan as normal or abnormal.

This work explores pathology detection with reduced annotation requirements, preserving the spatial context of volumetric CT data.

Method

A 3D adaptation of GLOW models the distribution of normal 48 × 48 × 48 CT patches. During inference, overlapping patches receive log-likelihood scores. Aggregation and smoothing produce a scan-level map; post-processing yields a patient-level prediction.

Training uses healthy patches. A separate validation set determines the decision threshold.

A 3D GLOW architecture uses invertible transformations to estimate patch likelihoods. The final normal/abnormal decision depends on spatial aggregation and a validation-selected threshold, not the patch score alone.

Experiments

The model learns from 500,000 normal 3D patches. Patient-level evaluation compares reconstruction, adversarial, flow-based, and diffusion-based approaches. The table below follows Table 1 of arXiv version 1.

  • Patch processing: volumetric patches avoid requiring a flow over an entire CT volume at once.

Conclusions & limitations

The results support learning normal pulmonary appearance for anomaly detection. The paper notes that aggregation needs further validation, especially when comparing 2D and 3D approaches. Extension to other organs and modalities remains future work.
